# If I owned MJG: what would make me read a redesign proposal

This is a role-play written from the owner's point of view to help shape the pitch. It isn't the real owner's opinion, so test it in a real conversation.

## What would get my attention right away

I'm busy, and I've probably been told before that I "need a new website". You'd have my attention if you:

1. **Showed me something I didn't know was broken.** Open my site on your phone and show me that my "More info" buttons do nothing, my About page is blank, and my form doesn't ask for a phone number.
2. **Talked about jobs, not websites.** I care about calls, quotes and booked cleans. Skip the talk about frameworks and code.
3. **Showed me who I'm losing to.** Put my site beside a local competitor showing insurance, testimonials and before-and-after photos.
4. **Gave me fixes today, before I paid.** Give me a short list I can use straight away, and I'll trust you with the bigger job.
5. **Showed me a mockup with my name on it.** Show me what my business could look like, using my number and my town.
6. **Kept the numbers honest.** Show me how the math works, without guaranteeing page one or a certain number of leads.
7. **Made the cost and effort clear.** Tell me the fixed price, the monthly cost, the launch date and how many hours you need from me.
8. **Made sure I own it.** I want to keep my domain, my content, my photos and my customer leads if we ever part ways.
9. **Showed me how I'll know it worked.** Send me a simple monthly report of calls, quote requests and reviews.
10. **Made changes easy.** I should be able to add photos and reviews from my phone without calling a developer.

## Top 10 improvements that directly increase revenue

Ranked by how directly each one should lead to booked work, and how soon.

| # | Improvement | How it makes me money | What the proposal should promise |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Tap-to-call everywhere** | Someone who needs a cleaner can reach me in one tap, before they try the next company. | Number in the header and hero, plus a fixed call button on phones. |
| 2 | **A short quote form that gets a phone number** | Fewer people give up, and I get a number I can call back. | Three fields to start, with the service already selected. |
| 3 | **Instant lead alerts and follow-ups** | I reach people while they're still deciding, and quotes stop being forgotten. | A text for every new lead, plus reminders at 2 and 7 days. |
| 4 | **A steady flow of real Google reviews** | People choose the company others vouch for, and reviews can help my Google ranking. | A simple review link and message I can send after every job, within Google's rules. |
| 5 | **A complete Google Business Profile** | People searching for cleaners nearby can find my number, reviews and photos. | Full setup, with weekly photo and update habits. |
| 6 | **A page for each service** | Each page gives Google something specific to show for searches like "deep cleaning Timmins". | Residential, commercial and deep cleaning pages at launch. |
| 7 | **A real before-and-after gallery** | Customers can see the result before they pay, which makes my price easier to accept. | A gallery I can update, plus a photo and permission checklist. |
| 8 | **A written guarantee and proof of insurance** | It lowers the risk of letting a stranger into a home or a workplace. | Clear guarantee wording, plus insurance and WSIB details once confirmed. |
| 9 | **A commercial page and one-page sales sheet** | One office contract can bring in steady monthly income. | A walkthrough offer, compliance details, references and scheduling options. |
| 10 | **Recurring cleaning plans and easy rebooking** | Regular customers are worth far more than one-time cleans, and they make my schedule more predictable. | Weekly, biweekly and monthly options, with a prompt after each one-time job. |

## Working out what this is worth

Don't promise a number. Fill this in with the owner, using their real figures:

```text
Extra leads per month
× share of leads that become customers
× average value of a customer
= extra revenue
```

Compare that with the cost of the site to answer the only question that matters: **how many extra jobs would pay for this?**

## What should make the proposal easy to say yes to

- A one-page summary, with the details behind it
- The quick fixes included up front
- Three clear milestones: fixes done, new site launched, first results reviewed
- One decision to make: "Do you want to go ahead?"
